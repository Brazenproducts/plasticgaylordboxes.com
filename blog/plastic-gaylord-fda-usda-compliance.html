FDA and USDA Compliance for Plastic Gaylord Boxes

Plasticgaylordboxes Editorial • October 2026

Compliance Is Non-Negotiable in Food-Grade Bulk Handling

When a gaylord box carries bulk food ingredients, raw protein, or produce, the container becomes part of your food safety system. Procurement teams sourcing bulk containers for food applications must confirm that every unit meets the relevant FDA and USDA standards before it enters the supply chain. A container that fails an audit can halt production, trigger recalls, and damage customer relationships far beyond the cost of the box itself.

This guide walks through what food-grade compliance actually means for plastic gaylords, which documentation to request, and how to evaluate a supplier. Reusable plastic gaylord boxes from Elipacko are built with FDA-compliant resin specifically to clear these requirements.

What FDA Compliance Requires

The FDA regulates materials that contact food under Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations. For polypropylene containers, the key reference is 21 CFR 177.1520, which governs olefin polymers intended for food contact. A compliant gaylord must be manufactured from resin that meets this standard, free of non-approved additives, colorants, or contaminants that could migrate into food.

  • Food-grade resin: virgin or approved PP meeting 21 CFR 177.1520.
  • Approved colorants: any pigments must themselves be food-contact approved.
  • No migration risk: the material must not transfer harmful substances under expected use conditions.
  • Documentation: a letter of compliance or declaration from the manufacturer.

USDA Considerations for Meat and Poultry

Facilities operating under USDA inspection, particularly meat and poultry plants, face additional expectations. Containers in these environments must be constructed from non-absorbent, easily cleanable materials that withstand repeated sanitation. Polypropylene is well suited here because it is non-porous, resists moisture absorption, and tolerates the hot water and sanitizing chemicals used in wash-down protocols. Smooth-walled PP gaylords eliminate the crevices where bacteria harbor in corrugated or wood alternatives.

For operations handling raw protein, pairing compliant gaylords with the right handling equipment matters. Many plants standardize on PP gaylords alongside PP corrugated boxes to maintain a consistent, cleanable material across the facility.

Documentation to Request From Suppliers

Before committing to any supplier, procurement should request specific paperwork. A verbal assurance is not enough for an audit. Ask for a letter of FDA compliance referencing 21 CFR 177.1520, resin specifications or a material data sheet, and confirmation of the manufacturing location. Traceability of the resin source strengthens your position in any food safety audit.

Cleanability and Ongoing Compliance

Compliance is not a one-time event at purchase. The container must remain compliant through its service life, which means it has to survive your sanitation protocols without degrading. Corrugated and wood containers break down under repeated washing and become harder to clean over time. Rigid PP gaylords maintain smooth, sealed surfaces that wash down reliably across hundreds of cycles, supporting consistent compliance from first use to retirement.

Making the Compliant Choice

For any operation handling food in bulk, gaylord selection is a food safety decision first and a cost decision second. Insist on FDA-compliant resin, request documentation referencing the correct CFR section, and confirm the material suits your wash-down requirements. Sourcing from a direct manufacturer that can provide full documentation and custom sizing removes the uncertainty and keeps your facility audit-ready.
